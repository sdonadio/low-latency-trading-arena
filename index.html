The learning path

Fifteen topics, two tracks, identical content

The material is taught in two formats. A 15-week track gives each topic its own session; a condensed 9-session track pairs adjacent topics into one longer meeting. Nothing is dropped and nothing is reordered — the pairs are exactly the ones that share a lab. Flip the switch and watch the fifteen cards regroup.

15 sessions · one topic each
Reading the pairs. Weeks 2+3 are one cost-model story (what an operation costs, then who owns the memory); 4+5 are the two halves of “stop calling the allocator”; 6+7 turn compile-time machinery into a real order book; 8+9 are the O(1) data structures and the memory model they need; 11+12 are the wire and the I/O that carries it; 13+14 are the last microseconds and the tail they hide in. Weeks 1, 10 and 15 stand alone in both tracks.

    This stage

    NIC → host

    Why p99.9, never the mean. A bot with a 4 µs median and a 900 µs p99.9 loses exactly the races that pay: the volatile ticks when every participant fires at once. A single heap allocation, one page fault, one cache miss on a cold branch — none of them move the median and all of them live in the tail. Week 14 is entirely about that sentence.

    Toolchain check

    Toolchain: RED · 0 of 5

    Ticks are kept only in this browser tab and nothing is sent anywhere. No GPU and no cloud account is needed for any topic in this course; a laptop and a release build are the whole lab.

    If something is red

    -std=c++20 is rejected

    The course uses designated initialisers, constexpr containers, concepts in a couple of places, and std::span. A 2019-vintage compiler will not do.

    Fix: GCC 11+ or Clang 13+. On macOS, Apple Clang from a current Xcode is fine; clang++ -std=c++20 -E -x c++ - < /dev/null is a one-line smoke test.

    Your benchmark reports 0 ns per operation

    You measured nothing. At -O2 the optimiser deletes work whose result is never read, and your timing loop is exactly that.

    Fix: sink every result through a doNotOptimize helper, and never benchmark a debug build. Week 2 does this properly.

    perf is not available

    perf is Linux-only, and even there it needs perf_event_paranoid relaxed. On macOS the equivalents are xctrace, sample and Instruments.

    Fix: do the sampling work on Linux (a container or a small VM is enough), or use the platform profiler — but keep the steady_clock harness as the number of record, because it is the one that matches the grader.

    The threaded version is slower than the single-threaded one

    Almost always false sharing: two hot variables landing on one 64-byte cache line, so two cores invalidate each other on every write.

    Fix: alignas(64) on each hot counter. Week 2 reproduces the regression and Week 9 explains the coherency traffic underneath it.

    What you will have built by the end

    • An order book in flat arrays with O(1) top-of-book and price-time priority.
    • A pool allocator and a lock-free SPSC ring, both with published ns/op numbers.
    • A hand-written binary parser for a market-data feed, with gap detection.
    • A bot whose tick-to-trade p99.9 you can defend from a flame graph.
